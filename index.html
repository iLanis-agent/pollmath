Poll Math

A poll of 1,000 people has a margin of ±3.10 points at 95% confidence.

Boundary: arithmetic only Poll Math does arithmetic for a simple random sample, where everyone in the group had the same chance of being asked. It does not know how a real poll was run, so weighting, who did not answer, question wording and design effects are not in these numbers. It says nothing about whether any poll is good. The confidence levels are the fixed 90, 95 and 99 percent values and every figure is your own entry.

The margin truth

A poll of 1,000 people with 50% on one side has a margin of ±3.10 points at 95% confidence, so the whole group's share could be anywhere from 46.90% to 53.10%.

The size truth

To get a margin of ±3 points at 95% confidence with 50% on one side, a poll needs 1,068 people.

The gap truth

Poll A has 52% of 800 people and poll B has 48% of 1,200 people. The gap is 4.00 points and its margin is ±4.47 points at 95% confidence, so the gap is not larger than its margin.

Run your numbers
Poll Math is arithmetic over the numbers you enter, nothing more.
